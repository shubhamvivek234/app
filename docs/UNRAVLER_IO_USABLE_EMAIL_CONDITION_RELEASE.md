## Bounded scope

`IF_EMAIL_AVAILABLE` now uses the same guarded decision/receipt mechanism as stored-field conditions. The worker takes the stable workspace suppression guard, uses a snapshot/majority transaction, fences current authority records, reads current email policy/suppression, and commits the branch or review hold with the completed sequence-job receipt. The previous separate email read and lead advancement are removed. An ordinary condition opens no sender/proxy/provider credentials, issues no HTTP request, creates no action intent and spends no physical request budget.

## Policy and failure behavior

Policy version 1 checks the current stored email with the existing normalizer, current verification restriction, opt-out and workspace email-suppression record. Missing/null/empty verification, `not_verified` and `valid` retain the historical provided-address policy. Missing/null/blank addresses or invalid address syntax choose No. Explicit `invalid`, `unknown`, `accept_all` and `disposable` verification states also choose No, as does an email-only opt-out or suppression. This is permission to choose a sequence branch under the stored policy; it is no authentication, fresh provider verification, delivery attestation or permission to send.

Malformed address types, malformed opt-out types and unrecognized/malformed verification states hold for review. They never silently become a negative branch. Malformed compound recipient identities also hold. Review holds use `waiting_trigger` with no due time, a sanitized reason and a completed decision receipt. No automatic reset/retry path for the held lead is added. Reply, workspace DNC, closed workspace, missing paid access, paused/deleted/replaced campaign or sender, changed authorization/sequence/generation, missing current `sequence` permission, schedule and restore-epoch failures continue to deny advancement.

This strict v1 evaluator governs this condition only. Existing `is_lead_email_available` consumers in physical send/finder paths retain their separate older policy behavior, including handling of unrecognized verification strings; this slice does not certify or unify those paths. A saved positive decision is historical evidence only. Any later physical action must recheck its current prerequisites and suppression. Preview remains an explicitly supplied usable-email assumption; it does not read or validate a real address, and it never supplies execution authority. Stored email-presence conditions remain separate.

## Identity, atomicity and recovery

New email-condition jobs have integer `email_condition_contract_version:1` and bind the deterministic workspace/stamp key, explicit nonlegacy enrollment, scalar IDs/revisions/generation and the original BSON-native lead/campaign/sequence/sender identities. Stored-field jobs retain their existing marker and receipt compatibility. Exactly one recognized condition marker is required; bool markers, dual markers, copied keys, old unstamped jobs, malformed stamps and replaced parents deny. The scheduler does not automatically adopt missing/legacy enrollments. This is a local code cutover, with no automatic production migration.

Before a separately authorized rollout, drain and replace old scheduler/worker binaries and verify one coherent fleet before using the new condition. New-code rejection cannot fence an already-running old binary. Preserve older jobs for review; do not bulk restamp them or adopt their missing evidence. Live actions stay disabled through deployment and operational verification.

Current lead-policy changes conflict with the transaction's lead write fence and are reevaluated on a definite transaction retry. Participating suppression writers share the stable workspace guard, so an absent matching suppression cannot race a committed decision. Raw/older writers outside that protocol remain a limitation. Standalone MongoDB cannot supply a fallback nontransactional branch. Final callback checks run before writes and after the last awaited write; permission can still expire while MongoDB acknowledges a commit, and later work must perform its own admission checks.

An email receipt adds condition kind and policy version to the existing versioned node/result/reason/next-node/original decision/due-time metadata. It copies neither the actual email nor verification fields. Reference labels can themselves contain personal data. Existing sequence-job erasure/retention applies; there is no new collection/index/TTL or fresh receipt-specific erasure certification. Enabled-dispatcher redelivery returns a valid original receipt without reevaluation, restarting its delay, granting permission or resuming a paused campaign. A corrupted kind/policy marker cannot fabricate recovery. Database acknowledgement uncertainty does not invent a result.

Existing F6 saved original positive acceptance outranks an exclusive negative-path email decision. Exact native-bound pending email jobs may be superseded inside the shared transaction. The returning worker preserves superseded status, and failures roll back the nested redirect. Applicable F6 rechecks may perform their separately guarded connection GET; the entire F6-scoped job is not claimed to be network-free. Older unstamped email jobs require review in F6 supersession too. Shared joins, terminal paths, accepted/unknown sends, unproven provider facts and generic connection/read/open resolvers remain outside this slice.
